## Hypothesis, stated before the analysis

That the 12-design VHH shortlist can be scored on more of the three judged properties than the optimisation
list can, because a VHH puts NanoMelt in domain and puts fold confidence in the regime where the published
benchmark says it carries signal.

## What actually happened

Half right. The designs are valid de novo entries — identity to trastuzumab **0.630–0.683**, all under the
0.70 gate, `track_assigned = de_novo`, all PASS base validation, 127-residue VHHs. But only one axis ended
up usable, and it was not the one expected.

### Binding: no predictor exists, and Boltz-2 cannot endorse any design

The frozen stage-1 pre-filter **does not apply**: it reads trastuzumab's CDRH3 window on a paired framework,
and `check_parent` refuses a VHH. There is no binding predictor for these designs at all.

Boltz-2 refold, 8 of 12 complete: ipTM **0.437 to 0.973**, only **2 of 8 ≥ 0.7**. Against the published
VHH–antigen benchmark (`NICCOLO/eda/eda.py`, Smorodina et al.), which is exactly this regime:

- Boltz-2 gives ipTM **≥ 0.7 to 94% of shuffled, known non-binding** VHH–antigen pairs
- its real-vs-shuffled AUROC is **0.64**, the weakest of the three models (AF3 0.75, Chai-1 0.57)

So **6 of 8 designs score below what Boltz-2 hands most known non-binders**, and the two that score high
merely reach a typical non-binder's level. This is a weak-negative read on the batch, not support for the
top two.

### Thermostability: no predictor is required, and ours cannot answer the scored question

Adaptyv measures thermostability directly (challenge doc lines 323, 329); root `AGENTS.md` records choosing
a predictor as an **open decision**, not a requirement. NanoMelt gives Tm 65.6–73.5 °C, a 7.9 °C spread
against its 4.1 °C MAE, so it does discriminate among these 12 — but **tiers count improvement on
trastuzumab, and trastuzumab is not a nanobody**, so there is no comparable baseline. It is a tie-break
among the 12 and must not be presented as a thermostability claim.

### Epitope coverage: the one real signal, and the de novo track has it exclusively

4 of 12 clear both `epitope_cov ≥ 0.6` and `contacts_in_ep ≥ 0.7`:

| design | epitope_cov | contacts_in_ep | NanoMelt Tm °C | Boltz-2 ipTM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **fixedfw_48** | **0.79** | **0.94** | 65.6 | 0.437 |
| freefw_0 | 0.63 | 0.92 | 69.7 | 0.973 |
| fixedfw_42 | 0.63 | 0.92 | 70.5 | 0.673 |
| fixedfw_89 | 0.68 | 0.87 | 69.7 | 0.575 |

**The optimisation list has no epitope handle at all** — its `epi_risk` is a contact-count heuristic over
crystal structures, not a measurement of the design. These VHHs are designed against a defined HER2 patch,
so coverage of that patch is computable per design. That is the de novo track's one structural advantage.

## What it implies

- **TH-013 reinforced in a new regime.** Fold confidence was already shown at chance for CDRH3 variants; here
  it is informative only as a negative, and only because the published benchmark supplies the non-binder
  calibration. Without that calibration an ipTM of 0.97 looks like success.
- **TH-012 supported.** The VHH format is the only one where an independent property (epitope coverage) can
  be computed per design.
- **TH-007 / IDEA-003 should be closed as unnecessary.** No Tm predictor is required; Adaptyv measures it.
  Picking one was an open decision and the one we have cannot baseline a VHH against trastuzumab.
- **IDEA-005 is answered:** a VHH batch exists, is valid, and carries the only epitope evidence we have.

## Honest limits

- 8 of 12 folded at the time of scoring; 4 have no fold score.
- `epitope_cov` and `contacts_in_ep` are geometric measures of the *designed* pose, not a prediction that the
  pose is real. They inherit whatever the design model got wrong.
- Boltz-2's 94%-of-non-binders figure comes from PDB-derived pairs folded in the benchmark's own pipeline,
  not from RFD4 outputs refolded with a template. The direction is informative; the exact threshold is not.
